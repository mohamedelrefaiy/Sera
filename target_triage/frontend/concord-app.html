Concord · mRNA × protein

When the transcript says one thing and the protein says another.

You perturbed the same gene in two screens — one reading messenger RNA, one reading the protein it makes — and they disagree. Concord is the workspace that tells you which reading to trust, what the evidence can and can’t prove, and the single experiment that settles it.

The problem

Two screens, one gene, two answers.

Functional-genomics screens measure a perturbation through whatever readout the assay reports. A transcript screen counts messenger RNA; a protein screen counts the protein itself. When they agree, the call is easy. When they split — a transcript hit that vanishes at the protein level, or a protein hit with no transcript signal — you’re left guessing which layer of biology is real and which is noise.

That gap is where good targets get abandoned and bad ones get chased. Concord exists to close it with evidence, not intuition.

mRNAThe transcript readout — how much messenger RNA the perturbation changed.
ProteinThe protein readout — what actually reached the functional layer of the cell.
How it works

Five deterministic stages. Code computes; the agent narrates.

Every number you see is produced by a pure Python core — the same target and data always yield the same result. Ask about a gene and it runs the same five stages, in order.

  1. Load the screen

    Raw CRISPR-screen data enters at one boundary and becomes immutable records. A schema maps each screen's own columns onto a shared shape, so Concord runs on this dataset — or a screen you bring. Signals a screen doesn't carry are marked absent, never faked as zero.

  2. Rank impact

    Each knockdown is scored by how broad and how strong its effect is, across resting and stimulated conditions (8 h and 48 h), with a flag for effects that appear in only one condition.

  3. Reconcile mRNA against protein — the keystone

    The two screens are crossed by biological direction and land in one of five honest verdicts. A sign conflict — both screens fire but disagree on direction — gets its own box rather than being smoothed into “replicated.” The disagreement is the finding, not noise to bury.

  4. Adversarially verify — try to refute it

    Each candidate is attacked with computed checks. Gates can reject it outright: is the knockdown real, powered, clean, and reproducible across the four donors and across independent guides? Independent published screens can only add corroboration, never manufacture it.

  5. Decide, and name the experiment

    A go / no-go brief that folds in druggability, disease relevance, and data quality — plus the single, concrete bench experiment that would settle the disagreement. A decision you can defend, with the next step already written.

The core computes; the agent narrates. Colour is meaning: mRNA and protein are the two data channels, blue-grey is a locked deterministic verdict.

The evidence

Two layers of evidence. One says it's real; the other says it's worth doing.

The screens tell you the biology is real and reproducible. The primary mRNA screen supplies the signal; two independent published screens corroborate it; and precomputed cross-donor and cross-guide correlations prove the effect holds up rather than riding on one lucky donor or one off-target guide.

The annotations tell you the target is worth acting on. Live lookups add whether the gene is druggable, whether human genetics ties it to immune disease, and whether a molecule against it is already in clinical trials. A gene has to clear both layers to earn “advance” — reproducible biology and an actionable target.

Why you can trust it

Code decides. The agents narrate and cite — they don’t decide.

Every verdict on this page is computed by deterministic code from the underlying data. The language model’s job is to explain that result and point to its sources — never to invent the number, the gene, or the citation. That division of labour is the whole design.

The agent — narrating

“At 48 hours post-stimulation the protein signal holds while the transcript falls away — trust the protein reading here.”
