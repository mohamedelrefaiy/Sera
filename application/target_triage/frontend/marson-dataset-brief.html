01 / THE DATASET

Causal, not correlational

Most genomics tells you which genes move together. Perturb-seq deletes each gene one at a time and reads out the whole transcriptome per cell — so you learn what each gene does, not just what it associates with.

Alex Marson's lab (Gladstone–UCSF Institute of Genomic Immunology) ran this in primary human CD4+ T cells — real donor cells, not an immortalized line — across three activation states (Rest, Stim8hr, Stim48hr), because a T cell's regulatory wiring rewires completely once it's activated. The processed evidence layer in data/marson_perturbseq/ carries the trust signals in every row: did the knockdown work, do the two guides agree, does the effect replicate across donors, is it a CRISPR off-target artifact.

Why this dataset fits an adversarial verifier

A naive tool ranks by effect size and stops. Because reproducibility columns (cross-guide, cross-donor, off-target flags) are baked into the DE tables, our verifier can refute a weak hit using evidence already in the data — a computation-grounded rejection, with numbers and provenance.

04 / THE VERIFIER HAS TEETH

NRAS: ranked #1 by raw score — rejected

The single most impressive figure in the run. NRAS tops a naive "sort by effect size" tool. Our verifier kills it because the effect doesn't replicate across donors and the two guides disagree. Full computed check record:
