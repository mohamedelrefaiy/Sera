Why this ordering matters — and this is the line you can say out loud on camera:

Autonomy is safe here because each step's only power is to reject a candidate — not to invent one. The loop can only remove things from the shortlist. It structurally cannot amplify into something dangerous, because no step produces anything actionable.

That's the whole trust argument. A "propose experiments" loop without a verifier gating every step is an error-amplifier — step 1's shaky guess becomes step 2's premise. Building the verifier first, and running autonomy through it, is what makes it a collaborator instead.

Plus a hard rule enforced by the loader: a plugin does not load unless its positive-control eval passes. That single invariant is what keeps "general" from rotting into "does everything, does nothing" — generality is only allowed where a control can gate it. T-cells is simply the first plugin.

Be honest about this · the one thing most likely to be wrong

Two screens proves the data-reading generalizes. It does not prove the refutation logic does.

Your rejection checks — donor-robustness, cross-guide correlation — are shaped by genetics screens. Schmidt2022 is still genomics; it reuses the same vocabulary. A truly different field (chemistry, protein structure) would need an entirely new set of refutation checks — and nothing yet shows writing those is cheap. That exact assumption is what sank the old science platforms: the framework was easy, the domain content was the hard part.

Cheapest way to test it → build a tiny non-biological toy plugin: rank integers, "reject if not prime," evidence = a lookup table. If the core runs green on genes and on numbers, it's domain-agnostic by construction. Costs an afternoon — and it's your Tuesday, not your Monday.
