05 The Whitespace — confirmed from three independent angles

No shipped project pairs Claude + MCP + drug-target triage / trial-evidence scoring

Three separate agents (Devpost sweep, clinical-trials sweep, MCP-tooling sweep) converged on the same finding. Adjacent efforts do something else:

  • Patient-to-trial matching — TrialGPT, MatchMiner-AI, TrialMatchAI. Not target-level.
  • EHR / FHIR workflows — MeldRx, HL7 Global AI Challenge, OOP. Not target-level.
  • Model-training on fixed datasets — PharmaHacks 2026 winners (binding affinity, toxicity). Not evidence-integration.
  • Patient-facing genomics explainers — BNFOthon variant dashboards. Not target-discovery.

Your specific twist — rolling ClinicalTrials.gov + genetic + dependency evidence up to the target level for prioritization — is done in research papers and production platforms, but not as a competition-shipped, controls-gated instrument. That's defensible novelty, not a gap.

Your differentiators (the gaps rivals leave open)

  • Deterministic, control-gated scoring under the agent. Every rival is retrieval-and-narrate or free-form multi-agent. None advertises a reproducible eval that gates a target's verdict. Your "nothing's done until the positive-control eval prints PASS" is uncopied.
  • DepMap + PrimeKG integration. Largely absent from Claude-native bio tools — an obvious edge for a genomics-strong solo builder.
  • A transparent, auditable verdict — not a chat log. Show the aggregated evidence table + the agent's reasoning + a control-validated score. Open Targets scores but doesn't reason; the agents reason but don't emit a defensible score.
  • Video-first live-run notebook (web/live.html). A demo/UX angle none of them have — they're CLIs, plugins, or papers.
Reproducibility is a real edge — Anthropic said so itself. Its own BioMysteryBench found ~half of Claude's hard-problem wins came via non-reproducible reasoning paths. A deterministic, auditable evidence trail directly answers a weakness Anthropic has publicly flagged.

09 The Playbook — a solo Build-track entry, this week

  1. Pick the Build track + one tractable target-triage flow you personally find annoying in genomics. Write the one-sentence problem statement first.
  2. Define "done" = the positive-control eval printing PASS. This is your Impact + Reproducibility proof and your demo punchline.
  3. Max the "Use of AI" axis: be genuinely agentic (multi-step reasoning) and expose your tools/data as an MCP server. This is the highest-leverage single move.
  4. Lean into DepMap + PrimeKG — the evidence sources rivals skip. Differentiate on genomics depth.
  5. Storyboard the ~90-second demo before coding; build the skeleton to that one flow. Cut anything off the demo path. (Overscoping is the #1 killer.)
  6. Use real Marson/Gladstone data, not toy data — directly scored in bio events, and it's the featured dataset.
  7. Hard-code/cache the happy path — mock slow APIs, keep the ClinicalTrials.gov cache warm (already done), screenshot fallbacks. Judges penalize demos that never reach the punchline.
  8. Record a tight video: problem (30%) → solution + live demo (70%) → the eval PASS on screen. Keep tech jargon under two sentences.
  9. Keep code clean and commented (its own scored axis); start from your existing base. Submit early evening, not the last hour.
Bottom line: the research doesn't tell you to change course — it tells you the course is right and where to sharpen. You sit in a genuine gap between "thin solo plugin" and "600-tool research platform": a focused, controls-validated, genomics-literate target-triage instrument, exposed over MCP, demoed video-first on the exact dataset the organizers chose to feature. That's a defensible pitch on every rubric axis at once.
